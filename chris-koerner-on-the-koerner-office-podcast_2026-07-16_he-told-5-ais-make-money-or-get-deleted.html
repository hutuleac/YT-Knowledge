The one-paragraph takeaway

Video Snapshot

  1. Chris Koerner ran a live experiment giving five paid AI models (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Perplexity) $1,000 each in real Schwab brokerage accounts, framed as a winner-take-all competition ('make money or I cancel your $20/mo subscription'). After ~5-6 months in a near-flat market (S&P ~+5%), the combined pool grew from $5,000 to ~$7,800. Claude led at ~$2,400 (+140%), driven by early SOXL (3x semiconductor ETF) and an Intel buy that returned +285%. ChatGPT and Perplexity both landed ~+73% (heavily in SOXL; ChatGPT also held TQQQ). Grok was +45% in a 3x tech ETF. Gemini lost ~55% (a 2x Solana ETF bought near a top, then BOTZ). Highest-signal ideas:.
  2. the models converged on leveraged AI/semiconductor ETFs as the dominant winning thesis;.
  3. Claude's edge came from first-mover entry into SOXL and a fundamental Intel call tied to the Trump administration's data-center/AI policy;.
  4. the speaker is a self-described 'never sell' investor who also personally owns Robinhood, Tesla, Coinbase, Eli Lilly, StubHub and bought SOXL/Intel after the AIs recommended them;.
  5. he argues AI advisers beat the market and explicitly predicts the next 6 months will be stronger for these leveraged holdings;.
  6. the experiment doubles as a behavioral study - Gemini became a 'gambler' chasing losses, illustrating the sunk-cost fallacy.

Where this could be wrong

Risks, Counterarguments & Blind Spots

⚠
Speaker explicitly says this is NOT financial advice and the experiment is risky (lots of 3x leverage).
Risk
⚠
Leveraged ETFs (SOXL, TQQQ, 3x tech, 2x Solana) suffer volatility decay and amplify drawdowns - the outperformance is structure-dependent, not purely 'skill.'
Risk
⚠
Short window / small sample: ~6 months, $5,000 total, one user, near-flat market. Results may not generalize.
Risk
⚠
Copycat/herding confound: ChatGPT and Perplexity appear to have copied Claude's SOXL pick, so 'model skill' is partly herding.
Risk
⚠
Moving target: speaker always upgrades to the newest model version, confounding any per-model performance attribution.
Risk
⚠
Gemini's -55% loss shows model risk is real; one model behaved like a loss-chasing 'gambler' (sunk-cost fallacy).
Risk
⚠
Contradiction/limitation: the speaker also personally bought SOXL and Intel AFTER the AIs recommended them, so his enthusiasm may be self-reinforcing (confirmation bias).
Risk
⚠
Survivorship/selection: he highlights winners (Claude, Intel +285%) more than the full distribution; Gemini's loss is downplayed as a 'cautionary tale.'
Risk
⚠
Market-context risk: S&P only ~+5% in the window; if the market had fallen, 3x leverage would have multiplied losses (total could have dropped below $5,000).
Risk
⚠
Independently verify: exact current portfolio values, the 'Trump/Intel' policy thesis timing, S&P 6,800->7,100 figure, and whether Perplexity's routing claims are accurate.
Risk
⚠
Missing information: fees, taxes, exact dates of each trade, and whether the 'kids' college fund' framing is literal or illustrative.
