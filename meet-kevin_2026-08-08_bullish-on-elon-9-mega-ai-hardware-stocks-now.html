Marvell and Lattice Semiconductor — The Custom-Silicon Runners-Up

Kevin's second and third favorites both benefit indirectly from Nvidia's own architecture choices rather than from a direct Elon order.

  • Marvell (MRVL) makes custom silicon/XPUs (ASICs) for hyperscalers like AWS and Microsoft; Nvidia's new NVLink Fusion architecture lets those XPUs interoperate with Nvidia GPUs and Vera CPUs instead of being locked out — Marvell is one of the first adopters (Google's TPUs have not yet joined), which Kevin frames as a structural advantage.
  • Relevance to the Elon thesis: Terrafab's D3 chips (orbital) and AI6 chips (FSD/Optimus) are likely custom Tesla-fabricated rather than Marvell parts, but rising NVLink Fusion adoption broadly makes it easier for buyers to keep purchasing Marvell's custom silicon without integration risk.
  • Long-shot optionality: co-packaged optics (CPOs) and an 'AI RAN' partnership with Nvidia to push inference workloads to 5G/6G base stations — a future call option, not a near-term driver.
  • Marvell valuation: fair value ~$370 (≈ 69% upside from current), forward growth ~35%/yr, current PEG ~1.43, EPS estimate $4 for Jan 2027, $191B market cap; also has a forecast $10B optics/custom-silicon revenue target if data centers shift from copper toward fiber optics.
  • Lattice Semiconductor (LSCC) makes FPGAs — 1-2 per server rack historically, expected to rise to 3-4 per rack; every GB300 and Vera Rubin rack carries embedded FPGA content. Server revenue is already 38% of Lattice's total and growing.
  • Lattice valuation is highly speculative: current PEG just 0.42 (PE ~61) assumes near-doubling of EPS; a slower 40-50% growth scenario implies ~1.5 PEG. High-case fair value $763, low-case $225 — no reliable 4-year estimate exists because the company is too small.
  • Per-rack economics for Lattice: individual FPGA chips cost $15-50 each, but a single fully-loaded server rack can contain $1,500-5,000 of Lattice hardware and firmware — small unit price, meaningful aggregate exposure.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Macro/Mini Hard — SpaceX/Elon AI-hardware buildout programs referenced as the source of the 13,000 (base case) to 68,800 (bull case) GB300 server-rack orders driving this whole thesis.
Terrafab — Elon/SpaceX-linked chip and hardware program covering orbital (D3) and Optimus/FSD (AI5/AI6) custom silicon, relevant to Marvell's NVLink Fusion optionality.
XPU / ASIC — Custom silicon chips (like Marvell's) built for a specific hyperscaler workload, as opposed to general-purpose Nvidia GPUs.
NVLink Fusion — Nvidia's architecture that lets third-party chips (like Marvell's XPUs) integrate with Nvidia GPUs and Vera CPUs inside the same data center, rather than being excluded.
PEG ratio — Price/earnings ratio divided by expected earnings growth rate — used throughout this video instead of plain PE because it accounts for growth; below 1.0 is generally considered cheap.
FPGA (Field-Programmable Gate Array) — A reconfigurable chip made by companies like Lattice Semiconductor; used in small quantities (1-4) per server rack for control/management functions.
800V DC architecture — A power-delivery standard tied to Nvidia's upcoming Vera Rubin generation; GB300 racks don't require it but can use it, making it a forward-looking positioning point for Vertiv and Eaton.
Free cash flow yield — Free cash flow divided by market cap; used to flag Enphase's ~7% yield as unusually high relative to the other hardware names in this list (mostly ~1-2%).
ADR (American Depositary Receipt) — A US-traded certificate representing shares of a foreign company (here, Schneider Electric); often represents a fraction of one underlying share, which is why Kevin divides Schneider's reported EPS by 5.
