Executive Summary

Visser's 100-name thematic basket is "built entirely around physical-world AI infrastructure" — not AI software, not hyperscalers. He has described it as a five-theme basket (Rack, Chemicals, Optical Fibers, Advanced Packaging, Power/Energy) plus five individual names (Eli Lilly, EWZ Brazil, Palantir, Silver, one unnamed), which later evolved into a six-theme structure on his exhaustion sheet (June 18, 2026): Whole Rack, Chemicals & Materials, Semicon Architecture, Optical & Interconnect, Energy & Infrastructure, Macro Satellites. It is the broad tier of a three-tier product (100-name index / 25-name concentrated / 10-name hyper-concentrated). Performance anchors: the basket made new all-time highs while the S&P returned to unchanged (April 2026), was ~+60% YTD (May 2026), and the concentrated 25-name version ran +68% YTD vs +9% for the S&P (May 2026).

What We Actually Know

The product structure (from his own words)

  • "I built a 100-name thematic portfolio and am now launching a concentrated 10-name and 25-name version" — June 2026 episode. Three tiers: 100-name index (broad), 25-name concentrated (+68% YTD vs +9% S&P as of May 2026), 10-name hyper-concentrated (higher beta).
  • Basket construction: "100 names across five themes" that made new all-time highs while the S&P returned to unchanged for the year (April 2026 episode): The Rack (semiconductors, networking, memory), Chemicals (commodity inputs for AI hardware), Optical Fibers (Corning and related), Advanced Packaging, Power/Energy (uranium, utilities, grid infrastructure — "approaching" new highs). Plus five individual names: Eli Lilly, EWZ (Brazil), Palantir, Silver, and one unnamed position.
  • The six-theme exhaustion sheet (June 18, 2026, shown publicly on video) reorganized the basket: Whole Rack · Chemicals & Materials · Semicon Architecture · Optical & Interconnect · Energy & Infrastructure · Macro Satellites.
  • His screening workflow: three Excel sheets (fundamental, technical, exhaustion/TDS) uploaded into any LLM; example screen "PEG <1, technical score >85, exhaustion low" returned "15 names across optical, semis, chemicals."
  • His framing: "This is not Web 2.0. AI is not a technology. It is electricity." The basket is the scarcity trade: commodities, semis, infrastructure, energy, critical minerals — versus the abundance trade (software, private credit) he is short/avoiding.

Theme-by-Theme Notes

Whole Rack — the core

His oldest and most public layer: NVIDIA (platform), Marvell (interconnect moat, now favored over memory), Micron (memory bottleneck — sold in June, repurchased a fifth of the May position in July below ~$850), Intel/AMD (the "agentic AI CPU shortage" — Intel started below $110, now +400% from lows), Western Digital, Lattice, Qualcomm, Teradyne. In April he warned "every component of the AI rack needs more supply — CPU, memory, switching, networking, power, cooling, storage."

Chemicals & Materials

Thematically the purest "physical world" expression: DuPont (breakout), Freeport (copper), and the exhaustion-sheet universe (Entegris 70.6, Merck KGaA at a perfect 100/100 technical score, Henkel 72.4, Cabot 70.3, Ibiden 70.6). ISM reported aluminum, electrical components, bearings, memory, rare earths all "up in price and in short supply." China's sulfuric-acid export ban hits fertilizer and copper.

Semicon Architecture

KLAC is the anchor — "ranked #1 above AVGO, NVDA, and ASML," "cheapest stock in the entire Visser 100," PEG 0.13 on peak-cycle earnings, WFE projected $140B+ in 2026. Memory scarcity longs (Samsung, SK Hynix, TSMC) live here; Samsung's $100B long-term supply agreement and the 10-day decline buy signal were cited in July. This theme carries the highest exhaustion outliers (ALAB 78.2, MKSI 77.7).

Optical & Interconnect

Corning is the cornerstone holding ("the portfolio's stock"), joined by Coherent (Jensen-named), AAOI (added July), Credo (44.2 — lowest exhaustion in the June basket), Astera Labs (78.2) and Lumentum (10-name candidate). The Cisco bandwidth-warning episode framed optical as the next bottleneck after memory.

Energy & Infrastructure

The defensive tail of the basket: Vistra and Constellation in the 10-name equal-weight IPP index ("if defense starts working in AI, you'll see Vistra and these IPPs"), Exxon/Chevron bought as hedges, Eaton (capital goods), Vertiv (power/cooling, $15B backlog +109% YoY), Caterpillar (54.7 exhaustion). Theme average exhaustion 21.1 — "cleanest entry zone." Uranium, natural gas, batteries (Currie's convergence point), silver and copper are the commodity underlays.

Macro Satellites

The sixth theme (5.1 avg exhaustion — the freshest names): the five individual basket names (Eli Lilly, EWZ, Palantir, Silver, one unnamed) plus his named longs Google (Gemini/TPUs), Merck US, the XBI biotech ETF, Tesla ("top conviction buy"), and Oracle ("pullback = attractive"). Notably excluded: hyperscalers as a group (he calls them the counterparty — "spenders to receivers" rotation), software (IGV — structurally short), and private credit.

What's Deliberately NOT in the Basket

  • Hyperscalers (MSFT, AMZN, META) — "the hyperscaler rotation: from spenders to receivers"; he prefers the suppliers. GOOGL is the exception (long).
  • Software / SaaS (IGV, CRM, WDAY) — structurally short; "AI is electricity, not Web 2.0."
  • Private credit (OWL, BX, CCLFX) — cautious/avoid; redemption-gate spiral narrative.
  • Financials (XLF, KRE) — below 200-DMA, worst sector; avoid bottom-fishing.
  • Crypto — separate allocation, not part of the 100: BTC (long, own directly over MSTR), ETH, SOL.
  • VIX / IWM / ex-US indices — macro overlay positions, not basket constituents.

Methodology & Confidence Tiers

Tier A — Confirmed (47): Explicitly named as a long, position, buy, or core holding in ≥1 episode recap or transcript. Dates and context are in the evidence column. This tier alone is a solid portfolio skeleton — every name is real.

Tier B — High (30): Present in his screening universe (exhaustion sheet scores or theme rankings from the June 18 sheet), in the 10-name candidate set, or required to make a described structure coherent (e.g., IPP index members, optical leaders). Near-certain members.

Tier C — Medium (23): Theme-fit inference: names that must exist in a 100-name physical-world basket given his repeatedly stated sub-themes (copper, uranium, silver miners, batteries, grid, packaging, power semis, WFE). Plausible but unverified — the true basket may substitute similar names.

Known gaps: The 5th individual basket name (unnamed); 2 of 3 names in the July "Fluence, Kimors, and Integris" mention; exact IPP index constituents beyond Vistra/Constellation; ~53 rows are inference. Anything could shift with portfolio updates — his June 18 exhaustion sheet showed him actively de-risking (sold MU, trimmed to 1/5).
