Crypto's Stall Is a Liquidity Story, Not a Broken-Tech Story

**Raoul's read on crypto 'chopping sideways':** it's not that the thesis failed, it's that Washington deliberately shifted the job of creating liquidity from the Fed/Treasury onto the banking system, and that handoff isn't finished yet.

  • Kevin Warsh, Scott Bessent, and Stephen Miller changed US banking regulations so liquidity creation shifts from central-bank balance sheets to the banking system itself; Japan ran the same playbook by steepening its yield curve, and banks started lending rather than the feared blowup.
  • Raoul's broad liquidity measure is growing ~4% but the economy needs ~8% to service debt and interest payments — enough to fund the AI bet specifically, not enough for everything else, which is why crypto stays starved of speculative capital.
  • The Iran war, sticky inflation, and the oil price have kept the yield curve too flat for banks to want to lend at the long end yet — the missing piece before the banking system fully takes over the liquidity job.
  • Raoul expects Fed governor Kevin Warsh to run Alan Greenspan's late-1990s playbook almost exactly: trim rates lightly, don't flinch at headline CPI, let productivity growth do the disinflationary work, and free up regulation so tech companies can build as fast as possible — a comparison he says Trump, Bessent, and Warsh have all referenced themselves.
  • Margin debt on US equities as a share of market cap is already elevated, which Raoul flags as evidence humans are already speculating even before this liquidity wave fully arrives.
  • Stablecoins are the release valve: Bessent's stated goal is $1-3 trillion in stablecoins, since that becomes a multi-trillion-dollar buyer of short-term US debt and extends dollar dominance to individual holders abroad.
  • Every bank and government regulating in crypto's favor (Clarity Act; Japan and Russia also moving) is, in Raoul's view, itself the signal — cites Larry Fink and Jamie Dimon publicly endorsing the direction — 'they wouldn't be regulating if it's not going to happen.'
  • Raoul personally knows Bessent from 30 years back (Soros era, when Raoul was at Goldman Sachs London) and notes Bessent used to subscribe to his research — 'we've got a macro hedge fund manager running the US Treasury, never happened before.'

Cape Ratio at 40x: Bubble Signal or Debasement Math?

**Tom's bear case and Raoul's rebuttal talk past each other on one number:** the S&P Cape ratio has roughly doubled its historical average, and the two disagree on whether that means danger.

  • Tom's case: Cape ratio historically averages 16-17x, is now ~40x (levels last seen before the dot-com bust); Warren Buffett has trimmed Apple, added to Google, and is sitting on record cash; OpenAI has already plateaued and Anthropic is burning capital faster than its revenue grows.
  • Raoul's rebuttal: the Cape ratio ignores currency debasement — debasement inflates price while earnings track GDP, so a structurally higher multiple since 2008's money-printing era is expected math, not proof of mania.
  • His hyperscaler-debt counter: these companies carry roughly 4% of market cap in debt against cash flow that dwarfs it — going cash-flow-negative reflects capex for compute buildout, not wasted spending.
  • He draws a hard line between the hyperscalers and the actually-leveraged entities — CoreWeave, Oracle, and various special-purpose vehicles — which he concedes 'could blow up' without dragging down the core players.
  • Contrasts today with the dot-com-era fiber-optics buildout, which he says was insanely leveraged with zero cash flow (all capex, no revenue) — a structurally different situation from cash-generative hyperscalers today.
  • If OpenAI collapsed: Raoul expects the US government to step in rather than let the AI race stall, its compute to be bought instantly by the fastest bidder, and regulators to block any single buyer (e.g. Google) from monopolizing the freed-up compute.
  • The broader capex buildout is only ~30% complete against 2030 plans, throttled by slow electricity permitting — Raoul's reason for thinking the cycle has real room left to run before any real reckoning.

Names and terms at a glance

Glossary & Entities

Cape ratio — A valuation measure comparing price to a smoothed, multi-year average of earnings; Tom cites today's ~40x reading against a 16-17x historical average as a bubble warning sign.
Liquidity vs. velocity of money — Liquidity is the total supply of dollars in the system regardless of how fast it moves; velocity is how quickly that same money changes hands — Raoul argues rising velocity (via stablecoins/crypto rails) can reduce the need to inject new liquidity.
Financial repression — Deliberately holding interest rates below the rate of economic growth or inflation so government debt shrinks relative to GDP over time — the mechanism Raoul says brought US debt-to-GDP down from over 100% post-WWII to roughly 15% by the late 1960s.
Jevons paradox — The pattern where making a resource more abundant and efficient increases total demand for it rather than reducing it — Raoul's explanation for why GPU rental prices keep rising even as compute capacity expands.
Reed's Law — A proposed law stating network value scales even faster than Metcalfe's Law (which already scales with the square of connected users) — described here as a 'double exponential' behind AI adoption curves.
K-shaped economy — A recovery or growth pattern where different segments of the population or economy diverge sharply — one group's fortunes rising while another's decline — rather than moving together.
